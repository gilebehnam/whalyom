Ratings for all four named competitors look good (reviews.md, indicative). So the attack is **fit** ("too much tool for this job"), not **quality** ("they are bad").

## 2. Unique attributes (verified only)
1. A client brief becomes a priced proposal (C2).
2. The proposal has scope, timeline and **price options** (C3).
3. E-signature (C4) and a **deposit request** (C5) are part of the proposal.
4. All of it sits in **one link** the client opens (C6).
5. A free plan with 3 proposals per month (C7), Pro at $19/month (C8), and a 14-day Pro trial (C10).
6. A Team plan at $49/month for 5 seats (C9).
7. Built for freelance designers, developers and marketers, and for 2-10 person agencies in the US, UK, CA and AU (C11).

Being distinct means the *combination* plus the narrow focus. Competitors may have each piece on its own. We can't claim that nobody else combines them until competitors are checked.

## 4. Best-fit buyers
- **Beachhead:** solo freelance web designers and developers who answer 2-6 client briefs a month and price per project (icp.md "Solo Sam"). They decide alone and sign up self-serve, which fits the 50-trial goal and the $0 budget.
- **Expansion:** 2-5 person design, dev or marketing agencies on the Team plan (icp.md "Agency Alex").
- **Tertiary:** freelance marketers selling tiered packages. Price options fit them naturally (C3), but they are the weakest-evidenced segment.
- **Poor fit (say so plainly):** buyers who need integrations (C17, none exist), a full CRM/invoicing/time-tracking suite, recurring retainer billing, payment processing beyond a deposit request (C15), EU or local-currency/VAT/GST handling (C13, C14), event or wedding businesses, or sales teams at larger companies.

## 5. Market category
**Recommended frame: "proposal app for freelancers and small agencies"**, with "brief-to-proposal" as the descriptor for how it works.
- Why: buyers already search for and understand "proposal software" and "proposal template" (competitors.md, a keyword hypothesis). Making up a new category would cost education spend we don't have ($0 cap).
- Positioning within the category: *focused* (one job, one link) against *suites* (Bonsai, HoneyBook) and *sales-document platforms* (Proposify, PandaDoc).
- Rejected frames: "freelancer CRM" or "all-in-one" (we lose on breadth, and it isn't true), "AI proposal writer" (crowded, and AI generation isn't a verified feature), "document automation" (PandaDoc's ground).
- Still a hypothesis: whether "brief-to-proposal" means anything to buyers. Test it in SERP data and interviews (E-O8-2).

## 6. Five positioning ideas

### Idea A: "From brief to signed and deposited, in one link" (the one-link flow) **RECOMMENDED**
- **Statement:** For freelancers and small agencies who answer client briefs, Quoteflow is a proposal app that turns a brief into a priced proposal with scope, timeline, price options, e-signature and a deposit request, all in one link. You don't need a suite or three separate tools to win the job.
- **Rationale:** It goes straight at the status quo, the biggest alternative (template + PDF + e-sign + invoice). It is built entirely from verified claims (C2-C6). It contrasts with suites on fit, not quality, and that matches the review evidence.
- **Supporting messages:** one link for the client · scope and price written down before work starts · signature and deposit request in the same place · try it free with 3 proposals a month (C7).
- **Advantage that enables it:** the verified feature set (C6) and the narrow focus. This is the most defensible idea right now.
- **Risk:** competitors also offer proposal + e-sign + payment. Without a speed proof (C12) the idea can sound like a feature list. Fix it with a demo video once one exists.

### Idea B: "Give clients options, not a take-it-or-leave-it price" (price options)
- **Statement:** For freelancers who hate guessing one number, Quoteflow is the proposal app built around price options. Your client picks the scope and price that fits, then signs and pays the deposit request in the same link.
- **Rationale:** It answers the underpricing and padding pain (voc.md). Price options (C3) are a verified feature that is easy to show, and good-better-best is a known pricing practice. It fits marketers who sell packages.
- **Supporting messages:** set 2-3 options per proposal · the client chooses, so the question is "which one", not "yes or no" · signature and deposit request follow straight away (C4-C6).
- **Advantage:** C3 + C6. Do **not** use the about-36% close-rate figure (an unverified vendor claim, voc.md).
- **Risk:** Proposify and PandaDoc likely support pricing tables (unverified). The idea is distinct as a lead message, not as a feature.

### Idea C: "Proposal software, minus the suite" (focus against bloat)
- **Statement:** For freelancers who want to win the project, not run back-office software, Quoteflow does one job: a brief becomes a priced, signable proposal with a deposit request. No CRM, invoicing or time tracking to set up.
- **Rationale:** It turns the gaps we really have (no integrations C17, no suite) into a reason to choose us. Setup complexity on suites is a recurring second-hand theme (reviews.md).
- **Supporting messages:** nothing to learn beyond the proposal · free plan, $19 Pro (C7, C8) · keep the invoicing tool you already use.
- **Advantage:** honest narrowness. It also qualifies out the poor-fit buyers early.
- **Risk:** "less" is a weak reason to switch on its own. Works best as a secondary message under A.

### Idea D: "Team proposals for small agencies: 5 seats, $49" (agency pricing)
- **Statement:** For 2-10 person agencies whose team members all send proposals, Quoteflow Team gives 5 seats for $49/month (C9), so everyone sends the same clear proposal with options, signature and a deposit request in one link.
- **Rationale:** Per-seat cost is a pain theme against Proposify, PandaDoc and Bonsai [2nd]. Our price is a verified fact we can state without naming a competitor.
- **Supporting messages:** 5 seats, one price (C9) · consistent proposals across the team · the same one-link flow (C6).
- **Advantage:** C9. **No** "cheaper than X" (C16 needs proof).
- **Risk:** extra-seat pricing and team features are unknown (profile.md). It is the expansion segment, not the beachhead. Hold until the owner confirms the plan limits.

### Idea E: "Proposals for freelancers in the UK, Canada and Australia, not just the US" (regional)
- **Statement:** For freelancers and small agencies in the US, UK, Canada and Australia (C11), Quoteflow is a proposal app built for your market from day one.
- **Rationale:** HoneyBook is described as US/CA only [2nd, unverified], so UK and AU freelancers may be under-served.
- **Advantage:** C11 only, for now.
- **Blockers:** C13 (EU), C14 (local currency, VAT/GST) and HoneyBook availability are all unverified. Without local currency or tax handling, "built for your market" is hollow. **Park it** until the owner answers C14 and the competitor check is done.

## 7. Recommended positioning (internal canvas)
- **Category:** proposal app for freelancers and small agencies.
- **Best-fit buyer:** solo freelance designers and developers who answer client briefs. Next: 2-10 person agencies.
- **Main alternative to beat:** the template + PDF + e-sign + invoice patchwork. Second: suites that are too big for the job.
- **Distinct:** brief to priced proposal with scope, timeline, price options, e-signature and a deposit request, in one link (C2-C6).
- **Value:** less rework per brief, a written scope before work starts, and fewer steps between the client's "yes" and the deposit request. (Directional value only, no measured outcomes.)
- **Proof available now:** the feature list and price list (C1-C11), plus a free plan to try (C7) and a 14-day trial (C10). Nothing else.
- **Message order:** A (lead) → B (how it works / differentiator) → C (why not a suite) → D (Team page only).

**Candidate headline drafts (internal, not for publishing):**

2. "Scope, price options, signature and deposit request. One link for your client."
3. "Proposals for freelancers, without the suite."
None of these uses "in minutes" (C12), "only", or any number besides prices.
