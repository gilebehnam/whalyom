## 2. Core job statement
When a client sends a brief, I want to turn it into a clear scope, timeline and price the client can accept in one link, so I can win the project and start with a deposit without chasing across tools.

Job map (solution-agnostic steps):
1. Understand the brief and decide what is in and out of scope.
2. Estimate effort and set a price (often with options).
3. Present it so the client understands and trusts it.
4. Get agreement and a signature.
5. Secure a deposit before starting.
6. Follow up if the client goes quiet.
Quoteflow's verified coverage: steps 2-5 as scope, timeline, price options, e-signature and deposit request in one link (C2-C6). Step 1 and step 6 are not verified features (expiry/follow-up is not in the feature list; do not promise it).

## 3. Customer jobs
Functional
- Turn a client brief into a written scope and timeline without starting from a blank page each time.
- Price a project, and offer the client a choice of price options.
- Get a client to sign and pay a deposit before work starts.
- Keep proposal, signature and deposit request from scattering across three tools.

Social
- Look professional and organised to a prospective client (audience: the client, who compares me with other bidders).
- Look like a business, not a hobbyist, when asking for a deposit (audience: client; for agencies, also their own team).
- Agencies: send consistent proposals across teammates (audience: partners and staff).

Emotional
- Seek: confidence that the price is right and the scope is protected.
- Avoid: the dread of a blank proposal doc; the anxiety of underpricing; the awkwardness of asking for money; resentment at unpaid extra work.
- Note: these are inferred from paraphrased themes (voc.md), not from quotes. Validate in interviews.

## 5. Gains
- Expectations: a proposal that arrives as one link the client can read, pick an option from, sign and pay a deposit against (C6).
- Savings: less rewriting per brief (speed claim "in minutes" is C12, needs-proof; use only after a timed demo).
- Adoption factors: free plan, 3 proposals/month (C7); 14-day Pro trial (C10); $19 Pro (C8); $49 for 5 seats (C9). A visible demo of the real flow. Card requirement for trial unknown (C19).
- Life improvement: spend evenings on client work, not proposal formatting; start projects with a deposit in hand.

## 6. Four forces of switching (hypotheses)
| Force | Content |
|---|---|
| Push (away from status quo) | Rewriting each proposal; unpaid extra work after vague scope; clients stalling between PDF, signature and invoice; lost deals after slow replies |
| Pull (to Quoteflow) | One link: scope, timeline, price options, signature, deposit request (C6); free plan and low entry price (C7-C9); a narrow tool, not a suite |
| Anxiety (about switching) | New and unknown vendor, no reviews or customers (C18); will it look as professional as my template; does it process deposits (C15, unknown); local currency, VAT/GST (C14, unknown); no integrations (C17); "I will lose my existing templates" |
| Habit (inertia) | A free Docs template that works well enough; existing e-sign and invoice tools already paid for; familiar suite |

Net read: push is real but moderate for solos with a working template; the biggest blocker is anxiety (trust, payments, regional fit), not pull. Free plan and trial lower anxiety; they do not remove the trust gap.

## 7. Triggers (the moment to switch)
Hypotheses to test in interviews:
- A brief arrives and the template feels too slow.
- A proposal was ignored or a client went quiet.
- Just burned by scope creep on the last project.
- Hit a price rise or limit on a current tool (second-hand, unverified).
- Starting out freelancing, or a solo hiring first help (Team plan).
- Client asks for pricing choices or a deposit and the current setup cannot do it neatly.

## 9. Messaging implications (draft angles, not copy)
1. Job-led headline family: "Brief in, proposal out" style, built on C2/C6. Avoid "in minutes" (C12).
2. Scope clarity angle: tie to the unpaid-extra-work pain, but do not promise Quoteflow prevents scope creep; say what the proposal contains (scope, timeline).
3. Price-options angle: let the client choose (C3). No close-rate stats (the 36% figure is vendor-only).
4. One-link angle: proposal, signature, deposit request together (C6). Say "deposit request", not "payments" (C15).
5. Anxiety reducers: free plan, 14-day trial, honest "no integrations" note (C17).
6. Do not: invent testimonials, claim speed, claim UK/AU advantage over HoneyBook, claim payout speed, claim expiry.
