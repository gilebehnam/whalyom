## Executive summary
Beachhead hypothesis: solo freelance web designers/developers and marketers, plus 2-5 person agencies, in the US and UK/CA/AU, who write a proposal per client brief and today stitch it together from docs, a PDF, a separate e-sign tool and a separate invoice. Quoteflow's verified offer is a brief turned into a priced proposal with scope, timeline, price options, e-signature and a deposit request in one link (C2-C6), with a free tier (C7) and low entry pricing (C8, C9). Start with the solo freelancer (free-to-Pro path, self-serve) and use the small agency as the Team-plan expansion.

## Customer profile (B2B small business, self-serve)

Firmographics
- Who: freelance designers, developers, marketers; agencies of 2-10 people (C11).
- Geography: US, UK, Canada, Australia (C11). EU unconfirmed (C13, needs-proof): treat as out of scope.
- Size: 1 person to 10. Revenue: unknown, not assumed.
- Model: project-based services where each client brief needs a custom scope and price.

Technographics (hypothesis)
- Current solution: Google Docs/Notion/Canva template or email plus PDF; separate e-sign tool; separate invoicing tool; or a suite (Bonsai, HoneyBook) or sales-doc tool (Proposify, PandaDoc). Competitor details unverified.
- Maturity: comfortable with SaaS, self-serve signup. No integrations exist (C17), so those who need a tool-stack fit are a poor early match.

Situational triggers (hypotheses to test in interviews)
- A new client brief or inbound lead arrives and a proposal is due.
- Just lost a deal or a client went quiet after a proposal.
- Burned by scope creep or unpaid extra work on the last project.
- Hit a limit or a price rise on a current tool (competitor complaints in voc.md are second-hand).
- Starting freelancing or going from solo to a first hire.
- Free plan fits low volume: 3 proposals/month (C7).

Qualifying
- Must-have: sends custom proposals to clients regularly; priced per project or retainer; wants client to sign and pay a deposit online.
- Nice-to-have: wants to offer tiered price options; works across the four launch countries.
- Disqualifying: needs integrations (C17), CRM/invoicing/time-tracking suite, larger sales teams (PandaDoc territory), event/wedding businesses (HoneyBook territory), EU or local-currency/VAT/GST handling until proven (C13, C14), payment processing beyond a deposit request (C15).

## Personas (hypothetical archetypes, not real people; names are labels only)

### 1. Solo Sam, the freelance designer/developer (primary, beachhead)
- Role: solo freelance web designer or developer. Plan fit: Free then Pro $19/month (C7, C8), 14-day Pro trial (C10).
- Bio (hypothesis): takes 2-6 client briefs a month, writes each proposal from scratch or a template, worries about underpricing.
- Goals: faster proposals; look professional; get paid a deposit before starting.
- Frustrations: rewriting scope and price each time; unpaid extra work; juggling a PDF, a signature tool and an invoice.
- Tools: Docs/Notion/Canva, an e-sign tool, an invoicing tool (assumed).
- Channels (assumed, validate): Reddit r/freelance and r/webdev, Google search for "proposal template", YouTube, X.
- Buying: self-serve, decides alone, fast, pays from own pocket so price-sensitive.
- Messaging: do say "one link: scope, price options, signature, deposit". Don't say "enterprise", "sales pipeline", or any unproven speed/ranking claim ("in minutes" is C12, needs-proof).
- Proof needed: a visible demo of the real proposal flow; free plan to try. No testimonials exist (C18).
- Objection: "A Docs template is free." Response (hypothesis): the template does not give price options, signing and a deposit request in one link (C6).
- Paraphrased theme: proposals eat time and pricing feels like guesswork (voc.md).

### 2. Agency Alex, the small-agency owner (secondary, Team plan)
- Role: founder/lead of a 2-10 person design, dev or marketing agency. Plan fit: Team $49/month for 5 seats (C9).
- Bio (hypothesis): writes or reviews most proposals, 1-2 teammates also send them, wants consistent output.
- Goals: consistent proposals across the team; price options to anchor deals; less founder time per proposal.
- Frustrations: per-seat cost on suites (voc.md [2nd], unverified); feature bloat; inconsistent proposals between teammates.
- Tools: a suite or document tool plus e-sign (assumed).
- Channels: Google search, LinkedIn, agency communities, peers.
- Buying: researches 1-2 weeks, may check with a partner, owns budget.
- Messaging: do say "5 seats for $49/month" (C9). Don't say "cheaper than X" (C16, needs-proof).
- Proof needed: a clear team workflow demo; named plan limits (unknown: price per extra seat, what Pro adds beyond volume).
- Objection: "We need it to connect to our CRM/invoicing." No integrations exist (C17): qualify out honestly.
- Paraphrased theme: per-user costs and complexity on suite tools (voc.md [2nd]).

### 3. Marketing Maya, the freelance marketer/consultant (tertiary)
- Role: freelance SEO, social or content marketer selling packages or retainers.
- Bio (hypothesis): sells tiered packages, so good/better/best price options (C3) fit naturally.
- Goals: present package tiers clearly; get a signed agreement and deposit before kickoff.
- Frustrations: scope drift on retainers; clients ignoring long proposal PDFs.
- Channels: LinkedIn, X, newsletters, Reddit r/freelance, Google search.
- Buying: self-serve, fast.
- Messaging: do say "price options your client can pick from" (C3). Don't promise retainer billing or recurring payments: not in the verified feature list.
- Objection: "I just need to invoice monthly." Not served: a deposit request is not recurring billing (C15). Qualify out.
- Note: this persona is the weakest-evidenced of the three; merge with Sam if interviews show the same behaviour.

## Beachhead choice
Solo Sam first: self-serve, free entry, shortest sales cycle, matches the trial-signup goal (50 in 30 days, product.json). Reachable at $0 via search, communities and directories (free mode). Agency Alex is the follow-on once Team-plan details are confirmed. This is a reasoned pick, not tested.

## Research templates
Interviews: target 10-15 (no customers, so all prospects: ~8 solo freelancers, ~4 small-agency owners, ~3 marketers). Questions: walk me through your last proposal start to finish; how long did it take; what tools touched it; what happened after you sent it; what do you do about scope creep; how did you choose price; what did you pay for tools; what would make you switch. Do not ask leading questions about Quoteflow features. Synthesis: themes with count n/N, invalidated assumptions, ICP adjustments.
Survey (after interviews, 30 responses directional, 100 for quantitative): screening (role, team size, proposals per month, country); frequency and minutes per proposal; current tools; satisfaction 1-10; top annoyance; willingness to pay (open); consent to follow up.
