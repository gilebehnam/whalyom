- **Google's Search Status Dashboard lists these broad core updates since 2025: March 2025 (began 13 Mar 2025), June 2025 (began 30 Jun 2025), December 2025 (began 11 Dec 2025), March 2026 (began 27 Mar 2026) and May 2026 (began 21 May 2026). Judge ranking changes against these windows before blaming on-site work.** (2026-09-24; official, high) [source](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history) `K-32f3af3f47`
- **Google ran four spam updates in 2026: March 2026 (began 24 Mar), June 2026 (began 24 Jun), August 2026 (began 18 Aug) and September 2026 (began 24 Sep, about two weeks long). The August 2025 spam update began 26 Aug 2025. A traffic drop starting in one of these windows points to a spam-policy issue, not a quality issue.** (2026-09-24; official, high) [source](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history) `K-5709edd54a`
- **On 24 Sep 2026 Google began rolling out multimodal search reporting globally in Search Console. A new multimodal search type filter appears in the Performance report for Search results and in the Generative AI features report. It covers image-based searches: Google Lens, Circle to Search on Android, image uploads to Google Search and Chrome's 'Search this image'. Data appears only if the site gets this traffic. Use it to judge how product and visual pages perform in image-led search.** (2026-09-24; official, high) [source](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/09/web-multimodal-in-sc) `K-5d35581406`
- **From 30 Aug 2026 Google enforces its site reputation abuse policy differently in the EEA, after talks with the European Commission. Outside the EEA, a manual action demotes the affected section of a site. Inside the EEA, the manual action's demotion doesn't apply, and the section may be separated so it ranks on its own. Hosting third-party content to exploit a trusted domain is still a policy violation everywhere. Announced 28 Aug 2026.** (2026-08-28; official, high) [source](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/08/update-site-reputation-policy) `K-630b418298`
- **Since 15 June 2026, 'back button hijacking' breaks Google's malicious practices spam policy. This means scripts, ad code or libraries that insert or replace browser history entries so users can't go back. Sites that do it can get manual actions or automated demotions. Announced 13 Apr 2026.** (2026-04-13; official, high) [source](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/04/back-button-hijacking) `K-4b9996d23e`
- **On 31 Mar 2026 Google moved its crawler IP range files from developers.google.com/search/apis/ipranges/ to developers.google.com/crawling/ipranges/, because the ranges cover more than Search crawlers. The old path still works for now but Google said it will redirect old locations to the new ones within 6 months. Update firewall rules, WAF allowlists, bot-verification scripts and log monitoring to read the new /crawling/ipranges/ path.** (2026-03-31; official, high) [source](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/03/crawler-ip-ranges) `K-1882f2d7c6`
- **Googlebot fetches only the first 2MB of an HTML page or other URL (PDFs: 64MB; crawlers with no stated limit: 15MB). The 2MB includes HTTP response headers (corrected 29 Oct 2026). Bytes past the cutoff are not fetched, rendered or indexed, and the truncated file is processed as if complete. Each script, stylesheet or XHR has its own 2MB counter. Keep HTML lean, move heavy inline CSS/JS to external files, and put title, meta robots, canonical and key structured data near the top. Published 31 Mar 2026.** (2026-03-31; official, high) [source](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/03/crawler-blog-post) `K-3604d88583`
- **Google released its first Discover core update on 5 Feb 2026, for English-language users in the US, with expansion to all countries and languages planned over the following months. Its aims are less clickbait, more local content from sites in the user's country, and more in-depth, original, timely content from sites with topic expertise.** (2026-02-05; official, high) [source](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/02/discover-core-update) `K-cdb2a6b8fe`
- **Since 10 Dec 2025 the Search Console Performance report (Search results, Google News and Discover) has a granularity drop-down: Daily (default), weekly or monthly. Weekly or monthly views smooth daily swings and make date-range comparisons fairer, because day-of-week mismatches stop distorting the chart. Exports may change file and tab names, some column headers and sort order to match the chosen granularity. Available globally to all properties.** (2025-12-10; official, high) [source](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/12/weekly-monthly-views-search-console) `K-cc230575cf`
- **From 4 Dec 2025 Google began a limited, experimental rollout of AI-powered configuration in the Search Console Performance report: you type the analysis you want in plain language and it sets filters (query, page, country, device, search appearance, date), comparisons and which of the four metrics show. It works only in the Search results report, not Discover or News, can't sort or export, and can misread requests, so always check the filters it applied before trusting the numbers.** (2025-12-04; official, high) [source](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/12/ai-powered-configuration) `K-034e6018c1`
- **Search Console's Performance report has a branded queries filter that splits data into branded and non-branded queries. An AI system classifies them, covering brand typos, all languages and product names. It was announced 20 Nov 2025 and has been available to all eligible sites since 11 Mar 2026. It works only for top-level properties with enough volume, and it doesn't affect ranking.** (2025-11-20; official, high) [source](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/11/search-console-branded-filter) `K-b367b3449f`
- **Since 17 Nov 2025 Search Console lets you add custom annotations to Performance report charts: right-click the chart, choose 'Add annotation', give a date and a note of up to 120 characters. Every user with access to the property can see them, so keep personal data out. Log migrations, template changes, content refreshes, new agencies or plugins, and outside events (holidays, core updates) so traffic changes can be tied to causes.** (2025-11-17; official, medium) [source](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/11/custom-chart-annotations) `K-83fbc7577f`
- **On 5 Nov 2025 Google said it is phasing out more rarely used search result features. From January 2026 Search Console and its API stop supporting the affected structured data types. Google lists specifics only in the Search documentation changelog, so check that changelog before adding niche schema types.** (2025-11-05; official, high) [source](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/11/update-on-our-efforts) `K-5e98d85976`
- **Search Console Insights has a 'Queries leading to your site' card with Query groups (announced 27 Oct 2025, gradual rollout). AI clusters phrasings, misspellings and languages with the same intent into groups showing total clicks, sorted into Top, Trending up and Trending down; clicking a group opens the Performance report with its queries. Only properties with a large query volume get it, groups can change over time, and they don't affect ranking. Use groups to plan topics, not exact keywords.** (2025-10-27; official, high) [source](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/10/search-console-query-groups) `K-25ed99b84d`
- **In June 2025 Google started phasing seven structured data features out of Search results: Book Actions, Course Info, ClaimReview, Estimated Salary, Learning Video, Special Announcement and Vehicle Listing. By 8 Sep 2025 all but Book Actions were gone from results. From 9 Sep 2025 they were removed from Search Console reporting and the Rich Results Test, and API support ran through December 2025. Don't build new pages expecting these rich results.** (2025-06-12; official, high) [source](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/06/simplifying-search-results) `K-60207063c4`
