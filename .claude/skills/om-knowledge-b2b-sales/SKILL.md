- **UK PECR B2B rule (ICO): you may email or text corporate subscribers (companies, LLPs, government bodies) without prior consent, but you should identify yourself and the ICO advises keeping a 'do not email or text' list of businesses that object. The ICO says this guidance is under review after the Data (Use and Access) Act and may change.** (2026-06-19; official, high) [source](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/) `K-4e537b8e1e`
- **France (CNIL): B2B email prospecting may rely on legitimate interest, without prior consent, if the message relates to the recipient's profession, and every message must identify the sender and offer a simple, free way to object. Generic addresses like info@ or contact@ aren't covered by these rules. Pre-ticked consent boxes are banned.** (2026-06-10; official, high) [source](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/la-prospection-commerciale-par-courrier-electronique) `K-2956d40395`

- **The FTC says CAN-SPAM makes no exception for business-to-business email. Every commercial email needs accurate header details, a subject line that doesn't mislead, a clear notice that it is an ad, a valid physical postal address and a clear opt-out covering all marketing mail. The opt-out must keep working for at least 30 days after sending, and requests must be honored within 10 business days. You stay liable for mail that third parties send for you. Each violating email can draw a penalty of up to $53,088.** (2025-01-17; official, high) [source](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) `K-49d6725733`
- **LinkedIn bans crawlers, bots, browser plug-ins and extensions that scrape LinkedIn, change how it looks, or automate activity. Its help page cites User Agreement section 8.2, which forbids bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services or to add or download contacts. Members who use such tools risk restriction or closure of their account. Treat LinkedIn outreach as manual work by the founder: no automated connection requests, profile visits, messages or scraping tools.** (2024-11-01; official, high) [source](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387) `K-83a5da0569`
- **LinkedIn Sponsored Messaging (Conversation and Message ads) has been able to target EU members since mid-October 2024, but only reaches EU members who opted in to ads in their inbox, and they can withdraw that consent. - **Australia's Spam Act (ACMA): every commercial email or SMS needs the recipient's express or inferred consent, and the sender must prove it. You can't send an email to ask for consent. Inferred consent needs an ongoing relationship the marketing directly relates to. A purchase alone doesn't count. Each message must identify the sender (legal name or ABN) with contact details valid for 30 days. The unsubscribe must be clear, free and need no login or extra data, work for 30 days, and be honored within 5 working days. Harvested lists are banned.** (2024-05-01; official, medium) [source](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam) `K-d73ff4d153`
- **Germany has no B2B exemption for cold email. UWG section 7(2) no. 2 treats any email advertising sent without the recipient's prior express consent as an unreasonable nuisance, whether the recipient is a business or a consumer. The only exception, section 7(3), covers an existing customer whose address you got through a sale: you may then advertise your own similar goods or services until they object, and at collection and in every email you must tell them they can object at any time. Don't send cold sequences to German addresses without consent.** (2021-05-28; official, high) [source](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__7.html) `K-f753edcf75`
- **GDPR recital 47 says direct marketing may be regarded as a legitimate interest, so B2B outreach to named people in the EU can rest on Art. 6(1)(f) where national e-privacy law allows it. Art. 21(2)-(3) gives an absolute right to object: once someone objects, stop all marketing processing of their data, profiling included. Art. 21(4) requires you to bring this right to their attention clearly and separately no later than the first communication, so put a plain opt-out line in the first cold email.** (2016-04-27; official, medium) [source](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679) `K-eec72c20fe`
