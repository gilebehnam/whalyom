# Executive storyline

**Build the pyramid first** (Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle*).
- The top is one answer to the governing question.
- Under it sit three or four supporting arguments, grouped MECE. Order them by importance, by time or by structure.
- Under each argument goes its evidence.
- Write this skeleton as plain sentences before you make any page.

**Opening: SCQA.**
- Situation: what the reader already accepts.
- Complication: what changed or what is at stake.
- Question: the question that follows from it.
- Answer: your recommendation.
- Keep it to five lines.

**Ghost deck.**
- Write every page title first, as a full-sentence finding with a number where possible. For example: "Paid search returns 3.1× on brand terms but 0.6× on generic terms."
- Read the titles alone, top to bottom. They must tell the whole story. If they don't, fix the story before you make any exhibit.

**Pages.**
- One message per page.
- The exhibit proves the title: the right chart for the comparison, labelled directly, with no decoration. Use the `dataviz` skill for charts.
- Put a source line and a date on every exhibit.
- If a page does not support the answer, cut it or move it to the appendix.

**Executive summary.** One page: the answer, the three arguments with their key numbers, the decision you need and the next steps.

**Decision page.**
- The options compared on the stated criteria, including "do nothing".
- The recommendation.
- Cost, value and risk.
- What the owner must approve.

**Style.**
- Short, active sentences in plain words.
- No jargon a smart outsider would trip on.
- Numbers rounded to what the evidence supports.
- Uncertainty stated, not hidden.
- Only claims from `claims.md` and cited facts.

**Final check.**
- Does the first line answer the question?
- Can a busy reader stop after the summary page?
- Is every number traceable?
