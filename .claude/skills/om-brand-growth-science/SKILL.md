# Brand growth science

Brands grow mainly by becoming easier to think of and easier to buy for more category buyers, not by deepening loyalty among a few. The research behind this comes from the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, the IPA effectiveness databank (Binet and Field) and the LinkedIn B2B Institute. Current figures and their samples live in `om-knowledge-brand-science`: read it first and cite the fact, never a remembered number.

**1. Who to reach.** Most sales come from light and occasional buyers. Plan reach across all category buyers before frequency on loyal ones. For B2B, most of the market is not in-market this quarter: brand work reaches the future buyers that performance work cannot.

**2. Mental availability.** List the category entry points (the situations, needs and moments in which buyers think of the category: who, what, when, where, why, with what, how they feel). Score how strongly the brand links to each one against two rivals, using a survey or review and search language. Build creative around the entry points that are large and under-owned.

**3. Distinctive brand assets.** Inventory colours, shapes, characters, sounds, taglines, fonts and faces. Score each on fame (share of category buyers who link it to the brand) and uniqueness (share who link it to no other brand). Use the strong ones in every ad and keep them consistent for years; retire or build the weak ones deliberately. Record them in `config/brand-tokens.json` and the brand guide.

**4. Physical availability.** Be easy to find and buy wherever buyers look: search, marketplaces, AI assistants, app stores, retail partners. Coverage and prominence first, then ease of purchase.

**5. Brand and activation.** Activation (targeted, offer-led, rational) drives short sales spikes; brand building (broad, emotional, memorable) drives long-term growth and price power. Set the split from the knowledge-base benchmark for the product's category and stage, state the time horizon, and never judge brand spend on last-click results.

**6. Measurement.**
- Brand health: prompted and unprompted awareness, entry-point linkage, asset fame, consideration, share of search.
- Effects: short-term sales lift from tests; long-term effects from MMM and base-sales trends.
- Report brand metrics on the same dashboard as revenue, with dates and sample sizes.

**7. Guardrails.** No fake reviews, invented awareness numbers or borrowed research figures without their source. Survey numbers carry their sample and margin of error. Budget changes are proposals for the owner.
