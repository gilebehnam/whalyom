# Green and ethical claims

Regulators treat vague or unproven environmental claims as misleading. The EU now restricts generic environmental claims and offset-based neutrality claims, the UK CMA and ASA apply the Green Claims Code, and the US FTC applies its Green Guides. Dates, scope and enforcement change: read `om-knowledge-ai-regulation` and `om-knowledge-privacy-law` (consumer-protection facts) first and cite the current rule.

**1. Find every claim.** Words (green, eco, sustainable, natural, clean, carbon neutral, net zero, climate positive, recyclable, plastic-free, ethical), symbols, colours or images that imply a benefit, and comparisons with other products or earlier versions.

**2. Test each claim.**
- Specific: what exactly is better, for which part (product, packaging, delivery, company), by how much.
- True and proven: evidence on file, current, covering the whole claim. Add it to claims.md with its proof or drop the claim.
- Whole life cycle: no claim that hides a bigger harm elsewhere.
- Comparisons: same basis, same period, the competitor or old version named or clearly identifiable.
- Future goals (net zero by a year): only with a published, independently checked plan and interim targets.

**3. Offsets and labels.**
- No claim that the product is neutral, reduced or positive because of offsets where the target market forbids it; describe the actual reductions and, separately, any contributions.
- Sustainability labels only from certification schemes or public authorities; never a self-made badge.

**4. Rewrite.** Replace generic words with the specific, proven fact and its scope ("packaging is 80% recycled cardboard, certified by …"), or remove it.

**5. Record.** Write the verdict per claim (keep, fix, remove) with the rule and evidence in the run file; blocked claims go to om-compliance-guard. Nothing goes live without the owner's approval.
