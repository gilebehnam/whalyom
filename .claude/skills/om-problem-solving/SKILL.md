# Structured problem solving

Use these seven steps, adapted from McKinsey practice and Conn & McLean, *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (2018).

1. **Define the problem.** Write a one-page problem statement before any analysis. It needs the governing question (specific, measurable, with a time frame), the context, the success criteria, the scope and what is out of scope, the constraints, the decision maker and the stakeholders. Get it agreed. A badly framed question wastes every step after it.
2. **Disaggregate.** Build a logic tree. Use an issue tree ("what are the parts of the question?") when you know little. Use a hypothesis tree ("what must be true for the answer to be X?") once you have a view. Every split must be MECE (mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive). Test it by asking what falls through the gaps and what is counted twice. Useful splits:
   - revenue = traffic × conversion × average order × frequency
   - profit = revenue − cost
   - growth = market momentum + share gain + new moves
   - customer journey stages
3. **Prioritize.** Score each branch on its impact on the answer and on your ability to influence it. Drop the low branches, say so explicitly, and record why.
4. **Plan the work.** For each remaining branch, write the hypothesis, the analysis that tests it, the data source, the owner agent, the deadline, and the result that would change the plan. Keep it to one page.
5. **Analyse.** Start with the simplest analysis that could falsify the hypothesis: back-of-the-envelope maths, a sample, one interview set. Escalate only if the answer is close. Use 80/20. Record every source and date.
6. **Synthesize.** Ask "so what?" of every finding until it becomes an implication for the decision. Group the implications into three or four arguments that support one answer (see `om-exec-storyline`). Then state what would make you wrong.
7. **Recommend and communicate.** Give the answer, the reasons, the evidence, the risks and the next steps, each with an owner, metric and date. Owner decisions go to approvals. Nothing outbound happens without approval.

**Check before handing back.**
- Could someone act on this tomorrow?
- Is every claim from `claims.md` or a cited source?
- Did you consider "do nothing"?
- Is the hardest objection answered?
