- **EU AI Act timeline: in force 1 Aug 2024. Prohibitions and AI literacy have applied since 2 Feb 2025, and general-purpose AI model obligations since 2 Aug 2025. Most of the Act, including the transparency rules, has applied since 2 Aug 2026. A ninth prohibition (non-consensual sexual content and CSAM generation) applies from 2 Dec 2026. The AI Omnibus (in force 27 Jul 2026) moved high-risk Annex III rules to 2 Dec 2027 and Annex I product rules to 2 Aug 2028.** (2026-07-27; official, high) [source](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai) `K-ac0b151d96`
- **The Digital Omnibus on AI amended AI Act Article 4 (paragraphs 1-3; the Service Desk page reflects the EUR-Lex consolidated version of 27 Jul 2026): providers and deployers must take measures to support AI literacy of staff and others operating AI systems on their behalf, considering their knowledge, experience and context of use, but the obligation does not require guaranteeing any specific level of AI literacy of any individual. Keep a short record of AI training and usage guidance for everyone (including agencies and freelancers) who runs AI tools for the brand.** (2026-07-27; official, high) [source](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-4) `K-b8276d6754`
- **AI Act Article 50 has applied since 2 Aug 2026. Deployers must clearly disclose deepfakes at first exposure. AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest must be labelled unless it had substantive human review and someone holds editorial responsibility (spell-checking doesn't count). The provider's machine-readable marks don't satisfy the deployer's duty. Providers of systems already on the market before 2 Aug 2026 have until 2 Dec 2026 for the Article 50(2) marking duty. Content made before 2 Aug 2026 doesn't need retroactive labels.** (2026-07-24; official, high) [source](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act) `K-32ab615321`
- **The Commission published the final Code of Practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content on 10 Jun 2026. It is voluntary and gives providers and deployers practical steps for AI Act transparency duties applying from 2 Aug 2026: label deepfakes and AI-generated or manipulated text on matters of public interest, and tell users when they interact with an AI system such as a chatbot. The page also links a resource titled 'EU Icons for labelling AI-generated content'.** (2026-06-10; official, high) [source](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-code-practice-marking-and-labelling-ai-generated-content) `K-2fdb0d3561`
- **Colorado SB26-189 (Automated Decision-Making Technology), signed 14 May 2026, repeals and reenacts the consumer protection provisions of SB 24-205. Deployers must give clear and conspicuous notice at the point of interaction with a covered ADMT and, within 30 days after a consequential decision with an adverse outcome, a plain-language description of the ADMT's role. 1 Jan 2027 is the start date for developer technical documentation duties. The Attorney General enforces through the Colorado Consumer Protection Act (no new private right of action); before 1 Jan 2030 the AG must give 60 days' notice and an opportunity to cure where cure is possible.** (2026-05-14; official, medium) [source](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-189) `K-76da58592e`
- **EU AI Act GPAI timeline (Commission GPAI guidelines page, updated 28 Apr 2026): obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models have applied since 2 Aug 2025 for models placed on the market after that date; the Commission's enforcement powers, including fines, apply from 2 Aug 2026; models placed on the market before 2 Aug 2025 must comply by 2 Aug 2027. The GPAI Code of Practice is a voluntary tool to show compliance. Per the page, only providers that make significant modifications to a model need to meet GPAI provider obligations; minor changes do not trigger them.** (2026-04-28; official, high) [source](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-gpai-providers) `K-d7d3c954b6`
- **The FTC's Consumer Review Rule (16 CFR Part 465, announced 14 Aug 2024) bans: fake or AI-generated reviews from people who don't exist or never used the product, reviews bought for a set sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled 'independent' review sites, review suppression by threats, and bought fake followers or views. On 22 Dec 2025 FTC staff sent warning letters to 10 companies, citing civil penalties of up to $53,088 per violation.** (2025-12-22; official, high) [source](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-warns-10-companies-about-possible-violations-agencys-new-consumer-review-rule) `K-e60346a7ff`
- **UK CAP/ASA position (29 May 2025): there is no blanket UK legal requirement to disclose AI use in ads; existing CAP and BCAP rules apply however content is generated, edited or targeted. Ask (1) would the audience be misled without disclosure, and (2) does the disclosure clarify or contradict the message. An AI image showing a product effect that does not match real-world results is likely misleading and a disclosure alone will not fix it; making clear that a deepfake is for comedy, or that an influencer is AI-generated, may negate an otherwise misleading impression. Minor post-production edits have long been used without always being disclosed, but context still matters.** (2025-05-29; official, high) [source](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/disclosure-of-ai-in-advertising-striking-the-balance-between-creativity-and-responsibility.html) `K-ace6dc2b24`
- **Since 6 Apr 2025 the CMA can decide consumer-law infringements itself and fine up to 10% of global turnover (5% plus daily penalties for breaching undertakings; 1% for information failures). The DMCC regime explicitly bans fake reviews and drip pricing (showing an initial price then adding fees during purchase). Show all mandatory fees upfront in UK prices and ads.** (2025-04-07; official, medium) [source](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-to-boost-consumer-and-business-confidence-as-new-consumer-protection-regime-comes-into-force) `K-ea2413a945`
- **UK DMCC Act banned practice on reviews (in force 6 Apr 2025; CMA guidance CMA208, 4 Apr 2025): it is automatically unfair to submit or commission fake reviews or reviews that conceal an incentive, and to publish reviews or review-derived information (star ratings) in a misleading way. 'Commissioning' includes incentivising by any means, and it applies to influencers, content creators and journalists too. Incentivised reviews are allowed only if the incentive is apparent.** (2025-04-04; official, high) [source](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews-cma208) `K-994f74ee60`
- **CMA208: any trader that publishes consumer reviews (own site, marketplace, social, review platform) has a positive duty to take reasonable and proportionate steps to prevent and remove banned reviews. Expected steps: a published plain-English policy prohibiting fake and concealed-incentive reviews, an evidence-led risk assessment reviewed over time, detection and removal processes, and not steering customers to complain instead of reviewing or suppressing negative reviews.** (2025-04-04; official, medium) [source](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf) `K-996f07c0a2`
- **FTC 'Operation AI Comply' (25 Sep 2024): five actions against deceptive AI claims. DoNotPay ('world's first robot lawyer'): proposed settlement requires a $193,000 payment and bars unsupported claims that it can replace professional services; Ascend Ecom (alleged consumer losses of at least $25M), Ecommerce Empire Builders and FBA Machine (alleged losses over $15.9M) were halted by courts over 'AI-powered' passive-income or earnings claims. Lesson: any 'AI-powered', 'replaces a lawyer/expert' or earnings claim needs substantiation in claims.md before use.** (2024-09-25; official, high) [source](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/ftc-announces-crackdown-deceptive-ai-claims-schemes) `K-048aa9c443`
- **In Operation AI Comply (25 Sep 2024) the FTC charged Rytr, whose AI writing assistant had a 'Testimonial & Review' feature generating detailed reviews likely to be false, with violating the FTC Act. The proposed order bars Rytr from advertising or selling services designed to generate consumer reviews or testimonials (3-2 vote, Commissioners Holyoak and Ferguson dissenting). Never use AI to draft reviews or testimonials attributed to customers; only publish real, consented customer words.** (2024-09-25; official, high) [source](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/ftc-announces-crackdown-deceptive-ai-claims-schemes) `K-7e82424adc`
- **AI Act Article 99 fines: prohibited practices (Art. 5) up to EUR 35M or 7% of worldwide annual turnover; most other operator obligations, including Article 50 transparency, up to EUR 15M or 3%; incorrect or misleading information to authorities up to EUR 7.5M or 1% (whichever is higher). For SMEs and start-ups each cap is the lower of the two amounts. Use the 3% tier when sizing the risk of an unlabelled deepfake or undisclosed chatbot in a campaign.** (2024-07-12; official, high) [source](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-99) `K-f332d2f6cc`
- **FTC endorsement FAQ disclosure rules: '#ad', 'Ad:' or 'ad' at the start of a post are about equal; 'Sponsored by XYZ' beats 'Sponsored'; disclosures in the middle or end of a post are easier to miss. Match the medium: visual endorsements need at least a visual disclosure, spoken ones an audible one, and both together is best; on TikTok superimpose large on-screen text. Livestreams need repeated or continuous disclosure. Add your own disclosure even when using a platform's paid-partnership tool.** (2023-06-29; official, high) [source](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) `K-40c36aac63`
- **FTC Endorsement Guides update (29 Jun 2023): 'endorsements' now covers fake reviews, virtual influencers and social media tags; new principle against procuring, suppressing, boosting or editing reviews to distort opinion; guidance on incentivised and employee reviews and fake negative reviews of competitors; a definition of 'clear and conspicuous'; and a platform's built-in disclosure tool may not be adequate. Advertisers, endorsers and intermediaries can all be liable.** (2023-06-29; official, high) [source](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/06/federal-trade-commission-announces-updated-advertising-guides-combat-deceptive-reviews-endorsements) `K-9813c0a530`
