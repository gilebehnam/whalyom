- **Nenycz-Thiel and Romaniuk (Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, B2B report, Nov 2025): physical availability means being easy to find and buy, with three parts: presence (being where buying happens), prominence (easy to find in physical, digital and mobile sales environments) and portfolio (products that fit current and future buyer needs). A survey of over 500 B2B marketers in the USA and UK found an average of 3.7 sales channels in use. Use: audit channel gaps against the category channel mix.** (2025-11-01; primary, high) [source](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/easy-to-find-being-where-b2b-buying-happens) `K-b13591a745`
- **Same Nenycz-Thiel and Romaniuk report (Nov 2025): its 'covering revenue' approach says presence should match each channel's value contribution to category transactions. Example: if 30% of category revenue comes through brand websites, the brand should aim to earn about 30% of its own revenue there. Presented as an approach with an example, not a fixed rule. Use: compare brand channel revenue mix with category channel mix.** (2025-11-01; primary, medium) [source](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/easy-to-find-being-where-b2b-buying-happens) `K-f710bceb03`
- **Kantar (BrandZ article, 7 Aug 2025; vendor-reported; Kantar BrandZ brand equity data combined with Kantar Worldpanel shopper behaviour, sample size not stated): brands that are 'Meaningfully Different' command five times the penetration of brands that are not. Just over half of the brands ranked in the 2006 BrandZ Global Top 100 remain on the list, and those that stayed had double the odds of strong emotive clarity. Use: add a meaningful-difference read to brand tracking, and note it is a Kantar model.** (2025-08-07; primary, medium) [source](https://www.kantar.com/Inspiration/Brands/A-different-route-to-growth) `K-8c8f727d00`
- **Romaniuk and Stocchi (Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, Aug 2025): among 2,835 professional generative-AI users in 13 countries benchmarking 20 AI providers, average brand rejection was 6% and 90% of rejection levels were under 10%; the highest was 15%. Unfamiliarity was the most common stated reason (22%), while cost and bad press were each 3% or less. Unaware or no-opinion users outnumbered rejectors for every brand (ratio 1.3 to 3.9). Use: for AI products, build mental availability before rebutting objections.** (2025-08-01; primary, high) [source](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/buyer-says-no-the-rejection-of-generative-ai-brands-in-b2b) `K-3ee889722f`
- **System1 (Test Your Ad page, 18 Jun 2025; vendor-reported): across 264 brands and 4,000+ ads, media investment alone (ESOV) predicts 27% of brand growth, and adding the System1 Star Rating raises that to 48%. The page says its database holds over 100,000 ads. No independent replication was found. Use: treat as a vendor claim; pair creative scoring with spend share, and hold out for validation.** (2025-06-18; primary, medium) [source](https://system1group.com/blog/test-your-ad-smarter-ad-analysis-for-campaigns-that-deliver) `K-13636903eb`
- **Binet and Field, 5 Principles of Growth in B2B Marketing (LinkedIn B2B Institute; analysis of B2B cases in the IPA Databank 1998-2018, case count not stated; page undated, 2025 footer): (1) share of voice above share of market predicts growth; (2) balance brand and activation, recommended around 50/50 for B2B; (3) acquisition beats loyalty and broad reach grows most; (4) maximise mental availability, with fame ranking highest as an objective; (5) emotional messages work better long-term, rational ones short-term. Use: use 50/50 as the B2B starting split.** (2025-01-01; primary, medium) [source](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/marketing-as-growth) `K-c0a179374f`
- **Romaniuk (Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, reported at Mumbrella360 2024; Mumbrella article dated 24 May 2024, the Ehrenberg-Bass copy shows no date): the fame of a distinctive brand asset is the proportion of category buyers who think of the brand when they experience the asset. It should be measured unprompted, because prompting gives inflated fame scores, by up to about 20 percentage points. An asset meant to stand in for the brand name should score as close to 100% fame as possible. Use: measure logo, colour, sound and shape assets unprompted before dropping the brand name from creative.** (2024-05-24; primary, medium) [source](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/mumbrella360-ehrenberg-bass-professor-says-youre-poorly-branding-if-youre-focusing-on-assets-over-your-brand) `K-9287286905`
- **IPA blog (13 Sep 2023): 'Effectiveness in Context' found the share of short-term campaigns in the IPA Effectiveness Databank was rising, and effectiveness, measured as the number of very large business effects reported in IPA cases, fell as that share grew. The data predate COVID and the cost-of-living squeeze. The original 2013 report has been cited in more than 70 academic papers and textbooks. Use: when a plan is mostly activation, expect fewer very large effects; sanity-check with a long-term brand measure.** (2023-09-13; primary, high) [source](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/the-next-chapter-for-the-long-and-the-short-of-it/) `K-4585e48b4a`
- **IPA blog by Hoad (13 Sep 2023) on Binet and Field's work: the 2013 report 'The Long and The Short of It' found an optimum brand-building to activation budget ratio of about 60:40, and the 2018 report 'Effectiveness in Context' found 62:38. The blog says the sweet spot stayed nearly constant across the reports. Use: use 60:40 as a starting prior and flex it, not as a fixed target.** (2023-09-13; primary, high) [source](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/the-next-chapter-for-the-long-and-the-short-of-it/) `K-ba4a34f6e0`
- **Romaniuk (Ehrenberg-Bass, July 2022), US business insurance survey (n=606) and 17 insurance products: brands with more linked category entry points had a lower likelihood of customers switching. The average slope was about -0.2, which the report reads as roughly 5% lower defection probability per extra linked entry point, significant in 16 of 17 products. In UK business banking (n=609) Barclays held 9.8% mental market share and 78% mental penetration. Use: treat entry-point breadth as a retention lever as well as an acquisition one.** (2022-07-01; primary, medium) [source](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/category-entry-points-in-a-business-to-business-b2b-world) `K-bd2e2ca22f`
- **Romaniuk (Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, B2B report, July 2022): category entry points (CEPs) are the internal and external cues buyers use to retrieve brands from memory in a buying situation. Generate them with the 'W' prompts (when, where, while, with what, why, how feeling, with or for whom), then prioritise with three tests, the '3 Cs': credibility (can the brand deliver), competitiveness (how crowded is the mental space) and commonality (how often it occurs and its value). A long-short list of 5 to 8 is suggested. Use: run CEP elicitation before writing a messaging platform.** (2022-07-01; primary, medium) [source](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/category-entry-points-in-a-business-to-business-b2b-world) `K-c003990f94`
- **Gelzinis, Kennedy, Beal, Hartnett and Sharp (Ehrenberg-Bass Institute; report to sponsors 2018, journal version online 21 Jun 2021): 70 brands in one Australian consumer goods category tracked over 20+ years, with 57 cases of all mass media stopped for a year or more. Sales index fell to 84 after one year (-16%), -25% after two and -36% after three. Smaller brands declined faster than medium and big brands; big brands (>1M units) appeared somewhat stable for 1-2 years. Of the growing and stable brands that resumed advertising, 5 of 10 kept their trend (2 of 5 growing, 3 of 5 stable); this is not all resuming brands. Use: model the cost of a media pause by brand size.** (2021-06-21; primary, medium) [source](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/what-happens-when-brands-stop-advertising) `K-d25f91adf4`
- **Ehrenberg-Bass Institute article on its LinkedIn B2B Institute study (Romaniuk, Dawes, Faghidno; 2021, sample sizes not stated on the page): in UK business banking, Barclays had 42% penetration of potential buyers, 27% of them solely loyal and 31% naming it their favourite; smaller Metro had 11% penetration, 3% solely loyal and 15% favourite. The article also says most B2B marketers believe growth comes from loyalty. Use: quote this as an illustration that big brands also have more loyal buyers.** (2021-05-01; primary, medium) [source](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/customer-acquisition-is-the-only-viable-growth-strategy-in-b2b-says-ehrenberg-basss-romaniuk) `K-3109b5ea20`
- **Romaniuk (Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, B2B report, May 2021) on the Duplication of Purchase law: brands share more customers with larger competitors, in proportion to the competitor's size. For 16 US business insurance products the sharing pattern correlated 93% with penetration; the same pattern appears in aviation fuel contracts and UK business banking switching. Sample sizes are not stated. The report concludes a brand's main competitors are the category's biggest brands regardless of positioning. Use: benchmark against category leaders, not look-alike niche rivals.** (2021-05-01; primary, high) [source](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/how-do-b2b-brands-compete) `K-871ef71a0b`
- **Dawes (Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, B2B report for the LinkedIn B2B Institute, May 2021): the '95-5 rule' is a rule of thumb from purchase frequency, not a precise law. If firms in a category switch provider about every five years, about 20% of buyers are in market over a year and about 5% in a quarter; at a two-year cycle it is about 50% a year and roughly 13% a quarter. Compute your own share from the category's interpurchase time. Use: set the in-market share from the purchase cycle before splitting budget between brand and activation.** (2021-05-01; primary, high) [source](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/advertising-effectiveness-and-the-95-5-rule-most-b2b-buyers-are-not-in-the-market-right-now) `K-ba48a6abe7`
- **Same Dawes report (Ehrenberg-Bass, May 2021; sample not stated on the page): many well-established B2B brands are linked to a given category entry point by only 20-30% of category buyers, and market leaders often reach about 50%; double-digit gains are described as a multi-year task. Advice: advertise to out-of-market buyers, spread spend across the year instead of one quarter, and track brand links to entry points as the mental availability measure. Use: set entry-point link-rate targets in years, not campaigns.** (2021-05-01; primary, medium) [source](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/advertising-effectiveness-and-the-95-5-rule-most-b2b-buyers-are-not-in-the-market-right-now) `K-c4b4209b3f`
- **Romaniuk, Dawes and Faghidno (Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, B2B report for the LinkedIn B2B Institute, May 2021): the Double Jeopardy law says smaller brands suffer twice, with fewer buyers who are also slightly less loyal, so brand size (penetration) drives loyalty rather than the reverse. First reported in the 1960s (McPhee, 1963); the report shows it across B2B datasets including UK business banking and US business insurance, and concludes that sustainable growth comes mainly from acquiring customers. Use: do not plan growth on loyalty gains alone.** (2021-05-01; primary, high) [source](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/the-double-jeopardy-law-in-b2b-shows-the-way-to-grow) `K-c4dca75da0`
- **Romaniuk (Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, B2B report, May 2021): among 609 UK business banking and 616 US business insurance buyers (22 banks, 17 insurers), brand rejection averaged about 10% (banking 11%, insurance 7%), against a B2C median of 9% over 535 brands in 24 categories. In a subset of 12 B2B brands, even the best-known had more unaware potential customers than rejectors (the per-brand table is an image). The report concludes lack of brand awareness is a bigger barrier to acquisition than non-customer brand rejection, a finding from that 12-brand subset, not a general law. Use: prioritise reach and entry points over rebuttal messaging.** (2021-05-01; primary, medium) [source](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/brand-rejection-in-b2b-indidence-reasons-and-implications) `K-d0c3ada1ae`
- **Nielsen (Marci, Oct 2017; vendor-reported), on a joint Nielsen Catalina Solutions and Nielsen study of TV and digital campaigns (number of campaigns and years not stated on the page): creative quality contributes as much to a brand's in-market success as all other factors combined; when creative is strong it drives up to 80% of in-market success on traditional TV and up to 89% on digital, and when creative is weak sales lift is weak and other media factors drive the result. Use: test creative quality before optimising targeting.** (2017-10-01; primary, medium) [source](https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2017/perspectives-want-a-successful-ad-get-creative/) `K-82a5fd8f6d`
