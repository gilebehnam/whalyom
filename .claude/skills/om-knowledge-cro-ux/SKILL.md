- **European Accessibility Act scope per Your Europe (checked 12 Jul 2026): requirements apply to products and services placed on the market after 28 Jun 2025, including online shops, ticketing platforms and banking services. Service businesses with fewer than 10 employees and under EUR 2 million turnover are exempt; self-service terminals in use before 28 Jun 2025 may continue up to 20 years. Any business can claim disproportionate burden only with evidence, reviewed every 5 years. Check whether the operator is a microenterprise before scoping an EAA audit.** (2026-07-12; official, high) [source](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/selling-in-eu/selling-goods-services/accessibility/index_en.htm) `K-8a0a4967a5`
- **US DOJ ADA Title II rule (Apr 2024) requires state and local government web content and mobile apps to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA. An Interim Final Rule published 20 Apr 2026 extended compliance by about a year: 26 Apr 2027 for entities with 50,000+ population, 26 Apr 2028 for smaller entities and special districts. It binds public entities, not private businesses, but it is the clearest US federal signal that WCAG 2.1 AA is the benchmark; vendors selling to governments need it.** (2026-04-20; official, high) [source](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/) `K-94033c2bcd`
- **Baymard's Cart & Checkout benchmark of 344 top-grossing US and EU e-commerce sites rates 65% 'mediocre' or worse; only 2% are 'good' and none 'perfect'. The research behind its 110+ checkout guidelines comes from 4,400+ moderated usability sessions and 12 quantitative studies with 20,240 participants. Even big brands' checkouts usually have fixable issues, so audit before assuming the checkout is fine.** (2026-01-01; primary, medium) [source](https://baymard.com/research/checkout-usability) `K-dd736a6c7e`
- **Baymard estimates that fixing solvable checkout design issues could raise a large e-commerce site's conversion rate by 35.26%, which on $738B of combined US and EU e-commerce sales equals about $260B in recoverable orders. Treat this as an upper bound for large sites, not a forecast for one test.** (2025-09-22; primary, medium) [source](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate) `K-482ca96d35`
- **Baymard: the average US checkout shows 23.48 form elements by default (14.88 form fields), while an ideal flow can be as short as 12-14 elements (7-8 fields). Count the store's default checkout elements and cut toward 12-14 by merging name fields, hiding company and address line 2 behind links, and defaulting billing to shipping address.** (2025-09-22; primary, high) [source](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate) `K-d99e2e243d`
- **Baymard's documented average online cart abandonment rate is 70.22%, averaged across 50 studies (page updated 22 Sep 2025). Use about 70% as the default baseline when no store data exists, and measure the store's own rate before claiming improvement.** (2025-09-22; primary, medium) [source](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate) `K-e49b1c9be8`
- **Baymard reasons US shoppers abandoned during checkout (excluding 'just browsing'): extra costs such as shipping, tax or fees 40%; slow delivery 20%; didn't trust the site with card details 19%; account creation required 18%; too long or complicated checkout 17%; site errors or crashes 17%; unsatisfactory return policy 13%; total cost not shown upfront 12%; card declined 10%; too few payment methods 9%. Prioritise upfront total cost, guest checkout and trust signals.** (2025-09-22; primary, high) [source](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate) `K-ec6b4ea6db`
- **The Commission's EAA page lists the services covered (e-commerce, banking, e-books, air/bus/rail/waterborne passenger transport, telephony, audio-visual media services) and products (computers and operating systems, ATMs, ticketing and check-in machines, smartphones, digital TV equipment). Member States had to transpose the Act into national law by June 2022.** (2025-06-28; official, high) [source](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/policies/justice-and-fundamental-rights/disability/union-equality-strategy-rights-persons-disabilities-2021-2030/european-accessibility-act_en) `K-8a48a5b4cb`
- **The European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882) has applied since 28 Jun 2025. It covers e-commerce, banking services, e-books, electronic communications, passenger transport services, computers, smartphones, e-readers and self-service terminals sold in the EU. Online shops and checkouts serving EU consumers must be accessible.** (2025-06-27; official, high) [source](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/eu-becomes-more-accessible-all) `K-bf180a9aae`
- **Interaction to Next Paint (INP) replaced First Input Delay as a Core Web Vital on 12 Mar 2024. Google's 'good' targets are LCP within 2.5 s, INP under 200 ms, and CLS below 0.1.** (2024-03-12; official, high) [source](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals) `K-0064c81bfa`
- **Search Console's Core Web Vitals report uses CrUX field data and judges each URL group at the 75th percentile of visits. Bands: LCP good <=2.5 s, poor >4 s; INP good <=200 ms, poor >500 ms; CLS good <=0.1, poor >0.25. A group's status is its worst metric (poor CLS + good INP = poor), similar pages are grouped, low-data groups fall back to the origin, and mobile and desktop are rated separately. Speed gates should check p75 field data on mobile, not lab scores alone.** (2024-03-12; official, high) [source](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9205520?hl=en) `K-8618cd998f`
- **WCAG 2.2 also adds two Level A criteria: 3.2.6 Consistent Help (help links, chat or contact details repeated across pages stay in the same relative order) and 3.3.7 Redundant Entry (information already entered in a process, such as a shipping address, is auto-filled or selectable instead of re-typed). Success criterion 4.1.1 Parsing is obsolete and removed. The AAA additions (e.g. 2.4.13 Focus Appearance: 2 CSS px perimeter, 3:1 contrast) are optional targets.** (2023-10-05; official, high) [source](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/) `K-713d92e80a`
- **WCAG 2.2 (W3C Recommendation, 5 Oct 2023) adds four Level AA criteria that matter for landing pages and checkout: 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (sticky headers, cookie banners and chat widgets must not fully hide the focused element), 2.5.7 Dragging Movements (offer a single-pointer alternative to sliders and drag), 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum) of 24x24 CSS px or enough spacing, and 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (no memorising or transcribing codes without an alternative such as paste or password managers).** (2023-10-05; official, high) [source](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/) `K-a259018e88`
- **NN/g eyetracking study (2018): users spent about 57% of page-viewing time above the fold (down from 80% in 2010) and about 74% within the first two screenfuls; about 81% falls within the first three screenfuls. Attention drops sharply after the fold, though people scroll when they have a reason. Put the value proposition and primary CTA in the first screen and avoid false floors. Limit: eyetracking of the tested pages, not a rule for every page type.** (2018-04-15; primary, medium) [source](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/) `K-5aff1316ff`
- **NN/g (Jakob Nielsen, 5 May 2008), from Weinreich et al.'s browser log study (25 users, 45,237 page views): on an average page visit users have time to read at most 28% of the words and more likely about 20%; each extra 100 words adds only about 4.4 seconds of visit time. Limits: a 2008 study with 25 above-average participants, many university employees, so treat it as directional. Front-load the offer and proof in headings, bullets and the first sentence of each section; don't rely on long body copy being read.** (2008-05-05; primary, medium) [source](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-little-do-users-read/) `K-46842ad648`
