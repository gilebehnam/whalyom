- **FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465, in effect 21 Oct 2024): it bans writing, selling or buying fake or false reviews and testimonials, including celebrity ones the business knew or should have known were false; offering incentives conditioned on a positive or negative sentiment; and insider reviews that hide the relationship to the business, where disclosure must be clear and conspicuous. Simply paying or rewarding for reviews is not banned; the sentiment condition is. Use: pay for reviews only without any sentiment requirement, and disclose staff and partner reviews.** (2024-10-21; official, high) [source](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers) `K-59c952a8e0`
- **FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465, in effect 21 Oct 2024): it also bars review suppression, meaning knowingly false accusations against reviewers, intimidation or unfounded legal threats to stop or remove reviews, and buying or distributing fake social media indicators such as bot followers when the business knew or should have known they were fake. Courts can impose civil penalties for knowing violations. Use: vet creators for bought followers and never threaten critics.** (2024-10-21; official, high) [source](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers) `K-bfb69f6279`
- **European Commission and national consumer authorities influencer sweep (fieldwork 2023, published Feb 2024): of 576 influencers screened across Member States, 97% (558) posted commercial content but only 20% (112) clearly said so, 38% (216) did not use platform labels such as Instagram's paid partnership toggle, and 62% were flagged for further investigation under national procedures. Use: assume EU regulators are actively checking creator disclosure; require platform labels plus a plain 'ad' marker.** (2024-02-14; official, medium) [source](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/consumers/consumer-rights-and-complaints/enforcement-consumer-protection/sweeps_en) `K-b9cff026ca`
- **FTC Endorsement Guides Q&A (Guides revised 2023; Q&A published 29 Jun 2023 per the FTC's resource listing and announcement; the Q&A page itself shows no date): an endorser must disclose a connection to the brand that a significant minority of viewers would not expect. Connections that generally count include payment, free products or trips, employment, family or friendship, and affiliate commissions. Endorsers who buy with their own money and have no connection generally need not disclose. Use: treat every affiliate link and gifted product as needing disclosure.** (2023-06-29; official, high) [source](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) `K-001c0bbd4d`
- **FTC Endorsement Guides Q&A: advertisers are responsible for what endorsers say for them, and handing the work to an agency does not remove that. The FTC advises training endorsers, explaining permitted claims, requiring disclosures, monitoring posts, and pre-approving content, with monitoring continuing for a reasonable time after a contract ends. Tagging a brand is an endorsement but not itself a disclosure; likes and shares may need disclosure inside a paid campaign. Use: add training, pre-approval and a post-contract monitoring window to every creator contract.** (2023-06-29; official, high) [source](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) `K-3fdbcc4d22`
- **FTC Endorsement Guides Q&A (Guides revised 2023): disclosures must be clear and conspicuous, close to the endorsement and hard to miss. A disclosure only in comments, a hyperlinked button or a video description is generally not enough; in video it should be in the video itself, ideally seen and heard, early, and repeated in long streams. Terms like 'Gifted', '#sponsored' or '#ambassador' can be unclear, while 'Ad', 'Paid ad' or 'Gifted by [brand]' are clearer. Platform disclosure tools do not replace the endorser's and brand's duty. Use: require on-video 'Ad' labels in creator briefs.** (2023-06-29; official, high) [source](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) `K-a018a3363d`
