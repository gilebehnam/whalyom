- **FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465, in effect 21 Oct 2024): it bans writing, selling or buying fake or false reviews and testimonials, including celebrity ones the business knew or should have known were false; offering incentives conditioned on a positive or negative sentiment; and insider reviews that hide the relationship to the business, where disclosure must be clear and conspicuous. Simply paying or rewarding for reviews is not banned; the sentiment condition is. Use: pay for reviews only without any sentiment requirement, and disclose staff and partner reviews.** (2024-10-21; official, high) [source](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers) `K-59c952a8e0`

Courts can impose civil penalties for knowing violations. Use: vet creators for bought followers and never threaten critics.** (2024-10-21; official, high) [source](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers) `K-bfb69f6279`

## From other topics that affect this department

- **The Universal Commerce Protocol (ucp.dev) is an open standard for agents, platforms and businesses, with specification, SDKs and reference implementations on GitHub under the Apache 2.0 license. Co-developers listed include Google, Shopify, Amazon, Walmart, Stripe, Booking.com and Marriott; Visa, Mastercard and PayPal are listed as endorsers. Capabilities are catalog search and lookup, cart building, identity linking (OAuth 2.0), checkout and order management; it runs over REST and JSON-RPC with support for AP2, A2A and MCP. Businesses remain merchant of record. A lodging spec is in draft and food ordering is coming soon.** (2026-11-03; official, medium) [source](https://ucp.dev/) `K-a1354620ad`
- **The current Model Context Protocol specification is version 2026-07-28 (JSON-RPC 2.0; hosts, clients, servers). Servers offer resources, prompts and tools; clients can offer elicitation; tasks, MCP Apps (inline UI) and Skills over MCP are opt-in extensions. Security principles: users must consent to all data access and operations, hosts must get explicit consent before invoking any tool, tools are treated as arbitrary code execution, and tool annotations are untrusted unless from a trusted server. MCP cannot enforce these; the host must.** (2026-07-28; official, high) [source](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/latest) `K-3d126d9271`
